# Premise

HACKASS was written to destroy the entire premise of hackathons.

The premise is not merely the event.

The premise is that software creation needs a stage, a timer, a judging panel, a sponsor rubric, a demo format, and a platform-controlled submission gate before an idea becomes real enough to matter.

HACKASS rejects that premise.

## What hackathons depended on

Hackathons depended on scarcity:

- scarcity of builders;
- scarcity of technical translation;
- scarcity of product judgment;
- scarcity of confidence;
- scarcity of usable development process;
- scarcity of people who can turn human intent into working software.

The event became valuable because the build process was inaccessible to most people.

That is the bottleneck HACKASS attacks.

## What changes

If a person can describe the software they need, answer the right questions, and be walked toward a working app, then the hackathon no longer owns the magic.

The magic was never the stage.

The magic was software creation.

HACKASS moves that magic out of the event and into the hands of the person with the idea.

## The thesis

Hackathons turned ideas into weekend demos.

HACKASS turns intent into software.

That makes the stage optional.

Then pointless.
